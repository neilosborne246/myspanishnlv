GHIC in Spain After Brexit: What Cover Do You Still Have?

What a UK GHIC covers, why it isn't NLV health insurance, and the healthcare routes for living in Spain.

Start Your Application ← All Guides

The Short Answer

A UK GHIC lets you use state healthcare during a visit to Spain when the treatment can't reasonably wait until you're back in the UK (GOV.UK GHIC guidance). It isn't a way to arrange healthcare for living in Spain, and it doesn't meet the NLV health insurance requirement. For living in Spain, your cover comes from one of the routes below — private health insurance, an S1, or another recognised entitlement — and whether a route is accepted for the visa is a separate check.

What the GHIC Covers

A UK GHIC (or a UK EHIC) gives access to state healthcare that becomes medically necessary during your visit — care that can't reasonably wait until you return to the UK. It is state healthcare only. According to the UK government, it doesn't replace travel and medical insurance and doesn't cover:

  • being flown back to the UK (medical repatriation)
  • treatment in a private medical facility
  • ski or mountain rescue

What the GHIC Doesn't Do for a Move to Spain

  • It's for visits, not residence. It covers care during a stay; it isn't the healthcare arrangement for the place you live.
  • It doesn't meet the NLV requirement. The consulate asks for health insurance from an insurer authorised in Spain, with cover equivalent to Spain's public system, no co-payments and validity for the whole visa period — or an accepted alternative. The Spanish consulate in London's checklist lists a registered S1 as that alternative. See the health insurance requirement.
  • It doesn't cover private hospitals or repatriation, as above.

Healthcare Routes for Living in Spain

The UK government's guidance on healthcare in Spain describes several routes. Which one applies depends on your situation; the S1 is one of them, not the only one.

  • S1 (UK-funded healthcare): you may be entitled to an S1 if you receive a UK State Pension or certain exportable benefits, or as a frontier worker. Once you register it with Spain's social security (INSS), you and your dependants get state healthcare on the same basis as a Spanish citizen, plus a UK-issued GHIC or EHIC for travel to other EU countries. See the S1 for British retirees.
  • Private health insurance: without an S1 or another accepted route, the visa requirement is met with a private policy that meets the conditions above. The UK government also notes that if you've lived in Spain for less than a year and can't get other cover, you'll need private insurance. NLV health insurance explains what to look for.
  • Convenio Especial: a public health scheme you pay into monthly, run through your regional health authority. At least a year on the padrón is one of its conditions, not the only one: it is for people who don't have public healthcare through another route, so check your eligibility with the regional health authority.
  • Permanent residence: once you're a permanent resident and the UK doesn't fund your healthcare, you can register with the INSS for state healthcare on the same basis as a Spanish citizen, showing a letter from NHS Overseas Healthcare Services that confirms the UK doesn't cover you.
  • Withdrawal Agreement: if you were living in Spain before 1 January 2021, you may be covered by the Withdrawal Agreement, which has its own healthcare rules.

Working and paying social security contributions in Spain is another route, but not one open to you on the NLV, which doesn't allow work.

Eligibility Is Not the Same as Meeting the NLV Requirement

Being entitled to public healthcare and meeting the NLV health insurance requirement are separate questions. The consulate decides what cover it accepts for the first application, and the Oficina de Extranjería decides for each renewal. Consulates ask for a private policy that meets the requirement; the Spanish consulate in London's checklist also accepts a registered S1. Before relying on any other public route for a renewal — the Convenio Especial included — check with the Oficina de Extranjería that it will be accepted.

Practical Checklist

  • Before you apply: work out which route covers you. If you think you're entitled to an S1, ask NHS Overseas Healthcare Services. Otherwise, get a policy that meets the visa requirement and check its certificate against your consulate's checklist.
  • After you arrive: register on the padrón (empadronamiento after arrival). If you have an S1, register it with the INSS.
  • For each renewal: keep your cover in force without gaps for the period you're renewing.
  • For travel outside Spain: check what your policy covers abroad. With a registered UK S1 you get a UK-issued GHIC or EHIC for other EU countries.

Sources: GOV.UK, ‘Apply for a free UK Global Health Insurance Card (GHIC)’ and ‘Healthcare in Spain’, checked 30 October 2026. Rules change; check the current guidance for your situation.
