How Spain Compares to the UK, US, and Australia

Spain consistently ranks among the most affordable countries in Western Europe for day-to-day living. While property prices and rents in major cities have increased in recent years — driven partly by increased expat and digital nomad demand — the overall cost of living remains significantly lower than comparable cities in the UK, US, or Australia.

As a general comparison, a comfortable lifestyle in Spain that would cost £3,500/month in London can typically be replicated for €2,000–€2,500/month in Madrid or Barcelona, and for as little as €1,400–€1,800/month in Málaga, Valencia, or smaller Andalusian cities. The savings are even more pronounced outside the major cities.

Key areas where Spain is particularly affordable compared to Anglophone countries include dining out, public transport, fresh produce, and healthcare (once you have access to the public system). Property-linked costs — rent in major cities, utility bills, and private health insurance — have risen in recent years but remain lower than UK or Australian equivalents.

Housing Costs by Region

Rent is typically your largest single monthly expense in Spain. Here is a realistic picture of what to expect across the most popular regions for NLV holders in 2026.

Madrid

Madrid has seen significant rent increases since 2021. A one-bedroom apartment in a central neighbourhood (Salamanca, Chueca, Malasaña) costs €1,300–€1,900/month. Slightly further out (Argüelles, Lavapiés, Embajadores) the same quality property costs €950–€1,400/month. Two-bedroom apartments in desirable areas run to €1,700–€2,800/month. Madrid offers excellent infrastructure, culture, and connectivity, but it is the most expensive city in mainland Spain for housing.

Barcelona

Barcelona is comparable to Madrid in housing costs and is arguably the most expensive city in Spain for renters. Central one-bedroom apartments in Eixample or Gracia typically cost €1,400–€2,000/month. Areas like Poblenou or Sant Martí offer slightly better value at €1,100–€1,600/month. The beach and Catalan cultural identity are significant draws, but housing costs make Barcelona challenging on a tight budget.

Málaga

Málaga has emerged as one of the most popular destinations for NLV holders and digital nomads. It offers an excellent climate, a growing international community, strong transport links (including direct flights from the UK, US, and Australia), and notably lower costs than Madrid or Barcelona. A one-bedroom apartment in central Málaga city costs €850–€1,300/month. In the surrounding Costa del Sol towns (Benalmádena, Fuengirola, Nerja) you can find good quality properties for €700–€1,100/month. Málaga is increasingly sought after and rents have risen, but it remains excellent value relative to Madrid or Barcelona.

Valencia

Valencia offers an outstanding quality of life at some of the most affordable big-city prices in Spain. One-bedroom apartments in desirable areas (Ruzafa, El Carmen, Benimaclet) cost €850–€1,200/month. Outer residential neighbourhoods start from €650/month. Valencia has world-class beaches, a thriving food scene, excellent public transport, and a large expat community. It is widely considered one of the best value cities in Europe for quality of life.

Smaller Towns and Rural Areas

If you are prepared to live outside a major city, costs fall dramatically. In smaller Andalusian towns (Ronda, Antequera, Úbeda), larger towns in the interior (Salamanca, Cáceres, Zamora), or rural areas, it is entirely possible to rent a good quality property for €450–€750/month. The trade-off is reduced English-language services, longer travel times, and sometimes fewer direct international flights. However, many NLV holders who prioritise cost savings, tranquillity, and an authentic Spanish lifestyle find smaller towns to be a rewarding choice.

Groceries and Food Shopping

Spain is excellent value for fresh food. Spanish supermarket chains like Mercadona, Lidl Spain, Día, and Carrefour offer high quality produce at prices significantly lower than UK or Australian equivalents. A weekly shop for one person can comfortably be done for €50–€80; for a couple, €80–€130.

Spain's mercados (local markets) offer outstanding value for fresh vegetables, fruit, fish, meat, and cheese — often at lower prices than supermarkets and with noticeably better quality. Shopping at the market rather than the supermarket is one of the easiest ways to eat well while spending less.

Budget approximately €250–€400/month for groceries for a couple who cooks regularly at home, including a reasonable amount of wine and Spanish beer.

Utilities

Utility costs in Spain are moderate. A typical monthly utility bill for a one-bedroom apartment includes:

  • Electricity: €60–€120/month (higher in summer with air conditioning, higher in winter with electric heating)
  • Water: €15–€35/month
  • Gas: €20–€60/month (if gas heating; not all apartments have gas)
  • Internet (fibre): €25–€45/month — Spain has excellent fibre coverage, and 600Mbps packages are widely available
  • Mobile phone: €10–€30/month for a good data plan

Total monthly utilities for a couple in a one-bedroom apartment: approximately €150–€280/month. Energy costs have fluctuated since 2021; budget conservatively.

Transport

Spain's public transport network is one of its great strengths. In major cities:

  • Madrid metro single ticket: €1.50–€2.00
  • Madrid monthly transport pass (all zones A): around €55
  • Barcelona T-Casual (10 journeys): around €11.35
  • Valencia monthly pass: around €40
  • Inter-city buses: €5–€25 for regional routes
  • RENFE (national rail) day trips: typically €10–€40 return

Many NLV holders in smaller cities or rural areas do own a car. Running costs include insurance (€400–€800/year for a typical car), petrol (around €1.60/litre in 2026), and an annual ITV (roadworthiness test) from around €35. Budget €100–€250/month for a car, depending on usage.

Dining Out and Leisure

Eating out in Spain is one of the great pleasures of expat life — and remarkably affordable. A Menu del Día (set lunch menu: starter, main, dessert, bread, and a drink) at a local restaurant typically costs €10–€15 per person and is one of the best-value meals in Europe. Dinner at a mid-range restaurant runs to €15–€30 per person excluding wine. A glass of decent house wine in a bar costs €2–€4.

A café con leche (coffee with milk) typically costs €1.20–€2.00 — significantly less than a London or Sydney equivalent.

Cinema tickets: €7–€12. Gym membership: €25–€50/month. Museum entry: typically €10–€15 (many free on Sundays). Spain offers a very high quality of leisure at a fraction of what equivalent activities cost in the UK, US, or Australia.

Healthcare Costs

As an NLV holder, your main healthcare cost is private health insurance, which is mandatory for the visa. Typical costs range from €50–€200/month per person depending on age and coverage level. See our complete guide to health insurance in Spain for full details.

Once you have private insurance, most healthcare costs are covered by your policy. Out-of-pocket costs that may arise include dental treatment (often not covered, or partially covered), optical care, and some specialist procedures not covered by your policy. Budget €0–€100/month for out-of-pocket healthcare depending on your health and your policy terms.

NLV Income Requirements and Cost of Living

Spain's NLV income requirements are set annually by reference to the IPREM (Indicador Público de Renta de Efectos Múltiples) — a Spanish public income reference index. As of 2026, applicants must demonstrate passive income of approximately:

  • Single applicant: approximately €2,400/month (400% of the monthly IPREM)
  • Each additional dependent (spouse, child): approximately €600/month additional (100% of the monthly IPREM per dependent)

A couple must therefore demonstrate approximately €3,000/month in passive income. As the table above shows, €3,000/month is broadly sufficient for a comfortable lifestyle in Málaga or Valencia, and a modest but perfectly comfortable lifestyle in smaller Spanish towns. For Madrid or Barcelona, it is achievable but allows limited luxury spending.

These figures represent the minimum the consulate requires — not necessarily what you need for your desired lifestyle. Most NLV specialists advise demonstrating income comfortably in excess of the minimum, both to satisfy the consulate and to genuinely enjoy your time in Spain.
