Convenio Especial: How NLV Holders Can Access Spain's Public Healthcare

A voluntary pay-in scheme giving legal residents access to Spain's public health system — but it does not replace the private insurance required for your NLV application.

Get Expert Help Back to Blog
Home› Blog› Convenio Especial Spain

After you have been living in Spain on your Non-Lucrative Visa for a year, passed through the residency registration process, and obtained your TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero), a new question emerges: what healthcare options are available to you as a legal resident who is not working and therefore not contributing to Spanish Social Security?

The answer that many long-term NLV holders discover — often with considerable relief — is the Convenio Especial. This is a voluntary scheme operated by the Spanish Social Security system (Tesorería General de la Seguridad Social, TGSS) that allows legal residents without automatic access to public healthcare to subscribe to Spain's Sistema Nacional de Salud (SNS) by paying a fixed monthly contribution. For under-65s, the cost in 2026 is approximately €60 per month. For those aged 65 and over, it is approximately €157 per month.

However, before we go further, there is a critical point that every NLV applicant and holder must understand. The Convenio Especial does not satisfy the private health insurance requirement for the Non-Lucrative Visa. This is the most common misconception we encounter. Read on to understand why — and how the Convenio Especial fits into your long-term healthcare planning in Spain.

The Critical Distinction: NLV Insurance vs. Convenio Especial

When you apply for the Spanish Non-Lucrative Visa, you must provide evidence of private health insurance that covers you in Spain without the right of repatriation, with no co-payments, and without significant exclusions. This is a mandatory visa requirement assessed by the Spanish consulate before they grant the NLV.

The Convenio Especial is a post-arrival arrangement for legal residents. It requires you to already have been living in Spain legally and continuously for at least 12 months (evidenced by your empadronamiento certificate). This means:

  • You can't use the Convenio Especial for your initial NLV application, because you won't yet meet its residence condition.
  • During your first year in Spain on the NLV, you must maintain your private health insurance policy.
  • From year two onwards — once you have at least a year's continuous residence in Spain, padrón registration in the region and no public cover through another route — you may be eligible to apply for the Convenio Especial.
  • Even after joining the Convenio Especial, check with your immigration specialist whether continuing your private health insurance policy is advisable for your NLV renewals, as consulate expectations on this point can vary.
Important: The Convenio Especial is useful AFTER you have your NLV and TIE and have completed 12 months of legal residence. It does not satisfy the health insurance requirement for your initial NLV application or first renewal. You still need Spanish-compliant private health insurance for those stages.

What Is the Convenio Especial?

The Convenio Especial (literally "special agreement") is a voluntary subscription to Spain's public Social Security healthcare system, regulated under Real Decreto 576/2013. It was designed specifically for people who are legally resident in Spain but who do not have automatic access to public healthcare because they are not in paid employment, not registered as self-employed (autónomo), and not receiving a pension that carries S1-form healthcare coverage.

In practice, this covers a significant number of NLV holders: retired people living off savings and investment income, people receiving pensions from non-EU countries that do not carry healthcare exportability to Spain, and adults living off passive income who have not yet built up entitlement to the public system through Social Security contributions.

Once you subscribe to the Convenio Especial, you are treated as a contributor to the Social Security system for healthcare purposes. You receive a health card (tarjeta sanitaria / SIP) and are registered with a primary care centre (centro de salud) in your municipality. You then access the full range of Spain's National Health System services in exactly the same way as any Spanish working contributor.

Who Is Eligible for the Convenio Especial?

To apply for the Convenio Especial in 2026, you must meet all of the following conditions:

  • Legal residency: You must hold a valid Spanish residency permit — for NLV holders, this means your TIE card. Tourists and short-stay visitors are not eligible.
  • A year's continuous residence, and padrón registration: You must show at least a year of continuous, effective residence in Spain immediately before you apply, and be registered on the padrón in a municipality of the region where you apply at the time you apply (RD 576/2013, art. 3). A historical padrón certificate (certificado de empadronamiento histórico) is the usual evidence of residence, but residence and padrón registration are separate conditions.
  • No existing Social Security healthcare entitlement: You must not already have access to Spanish public healthcare through employment, self-employment, or as a dependent family member of someone who does. You also cannot be covered under bilateral healthcare agreements — for example, UK and EU pensioners receiving an S1 form from their home country have their healthcare funded by that country and cannot additionally join the Convenio Especial.
  • Individual application: The agreement is strictly per person. It does not automatically cover spouses, partners, or children. Each family member who requires coverage must submit a separate application and pay a separate monthly contribution.

What Does the Convenio Especial Cover?

Once accepted, your Convenio Especial gives you access to the full range of primary and secondary care available under the Sistema Nacional de Salud. This includes:

  • GP (médico de cabecera / médico de familia) consultations at your assigned health centre
  • Specialist referrals through the public system
  • Hospital admission and surgery
  • Emergency care throughout Spain (and, with the European Health Insurance Card (EHIC/EHIC), emergency care in other EU countries)
  • Maternity and paediatric care
  • Mental health services
  • Pre-existing conditions: Unlike many private insurance policies, the Convenio Especial covers pre-existing medical conditions without exclusion periods. This is a significant advantage for people with chronic conditions who find private insurance either prohibitively expensive or unwilling to cover their existing diagnoses.

What Is Not Covered

There are some important gaps in Convenio Especial coverage that you should budget for separately:

  • Prescription medicines: Under the Convenio Especial, you pay 100% of the cost of prescription drugs at the pharmacy. Standard Social Security contributors pay subsidised rates (10–60% of cost depending on income). This is a meaningful difference for anyone on regular medication.
  • Dental care: Standard public healthcare in Spain covers very little dentistry — typically emergency extractions only. Routine dental care, fillings, crowns, and implants require either private dental insurance or out-of-pocket payment.
  • Optical care: Glasses and contact lenses are not covered by the public system.
  • Non-urgent transport: Scheduled non-emergency ambulance services are not covered.
  • Orthopaedic devices and prosthetics: Coverage is limited compared to standard SNS entitlement.

The Cost of the Convenio Especial in 2026

The monthly fee is set nationally by the Spanish government and is not means-tested — it does not vary based on your income, savings, or the value of your assets. For 2026, the rates are:

  • Under 65 years of age: approximately €60 per month
  • 65 years of age or older: approximately €157 per month

These figures are updated periodically by ministerial order. The over-65 rate is higher because older subscribers statistically use more healthcare services, and the scheme is designed to be self-sustaining.

For context, compare these costs to typical Spanish private health insurance premiums for NLV holders in 2026:

  • Age 35–50: approximately €80–150/month for comprehensive private coverage
  • Age 51–65: approximately €150–350/month depending on health history and provider
  • Age 65+: approximately €300–600+/month for private comprehensive coverage

For NLV holders aged 65 and over, the Convenio Especial at €157/month represents a very substantial saving compared to private health insurance at the same age. However, remember the prescription medication gap — if you are on multiple regular medications, factor in the 100% out-of-pocket pharmacy cost when comparing total healthcare expenditure.

How to Apply for the Convenio Especial

The application process is managed through Spain's Social Security offices (TGSS — Tesorería General de la Seguridad Social) or, in some autonomous communities, through the regional health authority. Because Spain's healthcare is decentralised, the precise process and forms vary by region.

Documents Required

Prepare the following for your application:

  • Valid passport
  • Your TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero) — the physical residency card issued after your NLV application and biometrics appointment in Spain
  • Certificado de empadronamiento histórico — your town hall registration certificate showing at least 12 months of continuous registration. Request this from your local Ayuntamiento (town hall); it is free.
  • Certificate of non-exportation of health rights — a formal document from your home country's healthcare authority confirming that you do not have public healthcare rights that are exportable to Spain. UK residents post-Brexit generally need to obtain this from the NHS. US, Australian, Canadian, and South African citizens typically obtain a declaration from their embassy or relevant government body.
  • Bank account details — payment is by direct debit (domiciliación bancaria) from a Spanish bank account.

Where to Apply

Book an appointment at your nearest TGSS office (oficina de la Tesorería General de la Seguridad Social). You can find your local office via the Social Security website. In some communities (notably Valencia and Catalonia), the application is handled by the regional health department rather than the TGSS directly. If you hold a digital certificate (certificado digital) or are registered for Cl@ve, you may be able to initiate the application online.

Processing and Activation

Once your application is accepted, you sign the Convenio Especial agreement and set up the direct debit. Coverage is typically activated within a few weeks. Your health centre assigns you a GP and you receive your tarjeta sanitaria (health card), known in some regions as the SIP card. Keep your TIE and health card together — you will need both when accessing healthcare services.

Convenio Especial vs. Private Health Insurance: How to Choose

For NLV holders who have completed their first year and are now eligible for the Convenio Especial, the decision of whether to switch from private insurance to the Convenio Especial (or to run both) involves several considerations:

Reasons to prioritise the Convenio Especial

  • You are over 65 and private insurance premiums are very high
  • You have pre-existing conditions that private insurers exclude or charge heavily for
  • You have established relationships with Spanish public healthcare providers
  • You plan to remain in Spain long-term and want to build public system familiarity

Reasons to maintain private health insurance

  • Your NLV renewal documentation expects to see private insurance (confirm with your immigration specialist)
  • You want faster access to specialists without public system waiting times
  • You want English-speaking doctors and private hospital access
  • Dental and optical coverage is important to you
  • You spend significant time outside Spain and need international coverage

Some NLV holders run both in parallel: the Convenio Especial for access to the public system (especially valuable for emergency and hospital care), supplemented by a lower-cost private policy for dental, optical, faster specialist access, and international coverage. The combined cost can still be lower than a premium private-only policy for older residents.

Regional Variations Across Spain

While the Convenio Especial is a national scheme with nationally-set costs, its administration varies by autonomous community. In Valencia, the Conselleria de Sanitat manages the process and issues the SIP card on approval. In Andalusia, the Servicio Andaluz de Salud handles regional registration. In Catalonia, the CatSalut manages the application. In Madrid, the Comunidad de Madrid's public health service oversees registration.

In some regions, you may submit your application at your local primary health centre (centro de salud) directly after the TGSS has processed your Convenio Especial agreement. In others, the health centre registration follows automatically from Social Security approval. Your local TGSS office will guide you through the specific steps for your region.

One consistent factor across all regions: the 12-month continuous empadronamiento requirement is strictly enforced. Gaps in your town hall registration — even brief ones — can reset the clock. Ensure your empadronamiento is kept up to date throughout your residency and renew it promptly if your address changes.
