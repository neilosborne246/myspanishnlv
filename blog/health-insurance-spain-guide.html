Everything you need to know about public and private health insurance in Spain, the top providers, costs, and the specific requirements for NLV applicants.

Why Health Insurance is Non-Negotiable for NLV Applicants

Private health insurance is one of the mandatory requirements for the Spanish Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV). Unlike EU citizens who may access Spain's public healthcare through their home country's reciprocal arrangements, non-EU nationals applying for the NLV must demonstrate they have comprehensive private health coverage for their entire stay in Spain.

This is not a formality. The Spanish consulate will scrutinise your insurance policy carefully. Inadequate or incorrectly structured coverage is one of the most common reasons NLV applications are rejected or delayed. Understanding exactly what is required — and which policies meet those requirements — is therefore essential before you apply.

What the NLV Requires from Your Health Insurance Policy

The requirements for NLV health insurance are specific. Your policy must:

  • Cover the full duration of your visa period — at minimum one year for the initial NLV
  • Have no copayments (copagos) — the consulate requires a policy with zero or negligible copayments; policies where you pay a sum at each appointment are typically not accepted
  • Have no exclusions for pre-existing conditions — or at minimum, the consulate requires the policy not to exclude general coverage (some nuance applies here by consulate)
  • Be issued by an insurer authorised to operate in Spain — international travel insurance policies are not accepted
  • Cover you for all medical eventualities — including hospitalisation, surgery, and emergency care
  • Cover Spain specifically — not just as part of a Schengen-wide or worldwide plan

Some consulates also have minimum coverage amount requirements. Practice varies by consulate — requirements in Los Angeles may differ from those in Sydney or London. This is why getting specialist advice before purchasing your policy is important. The wrong policy can mean your application fails.

Spain's Public Healthcare System: What Expats Need to Know

Spain operates the Sistema Nacional de Salud (SNS) — a public healthcare system consistently ranked among the best in Europe. The SNS is funded through social security contributions and provides universal coverage for registered residents who contribute to the system through employment or self-employment.

As an NLV holder, you are not initially entitled to use the public healthcare system. NLV holders do not work in Spain, so they do not make social security contributions, and they therefore cannot access SNS as of right when they first arrive. This is precisely why private health insurance is required for the visa.

However, once you have been in Spain for one year and have renewed your NLV, some regions allow you to access the public system through a mechanism called the Convenio Especial — an agreement where you pay a monthly contribution to join the SNS. Costs are approximately €60 per month for adults under 65 and €157 per month for those aged 65 or over. Coverage through the Convenio Especial is comprehensive but does not replace private insurance for visa purposes.

Public vs Private Healthcare: A Comparison for NLV Holders

Understanding the difference between public and private care helps you make an informed decision about your coverage:

Public Healthcare (SNS)

  • Comprehensive coverage at minimal cost once accessed
  • Long waiting times for non-urgent specialist appointments — sometimes weeks or months
  • Excellent emergency care — A&E departments are world-class
  • Prescription medicines are subsidised (usually 40–60% discount)
  • Dental and optical care largely excluded (except emergencies)
  • Language barrier can be an issue outside major cities

Private Healthcare

  • Faster access to specialists — often within days
  • More English-speaking doctors available, particularly in expat areas
  • Modern private hospitals with shorter waiting times for procedures
  • More comfortable facilities and often more personalised care
  • Dental and optical care can be included or added as extras
  • Required for the NLV regardless of other coverage

Best Private Health Insurance Providers for NLV Holders

The following providers are among the most commonly used by NLV applicants and expats in Spain. Each has been successfully accepted by Spanish consulates. Always confirm the specific policy wording meets your consulate's requirements before purchasing.

Sanitas

Sanitas is owned by BUPA and is one of the largest private health insurers in Spain with an extensive network of hospitals, clinics, and specialists across the country. It is widely accepted by Spanish consulates for NLV applications. Sanitas policies can be purchased in English, and the company has dedicated expat teams. Its network is particularly strong in Madrid, Barcelona, the Costa del Sol, and the Costa Blanca. Approximate annual cost: €800–€2,500 depending on age and coverage level.

AXA Spain

AXA is a global insurer with a strong presence in Spain. Its Spanish subsidiary offers comprehensive health plans that meet NLV requirements, including policies with no copayments. AXA is often competitive on price for younger applicants and has a wide network of providers. Customer service is available in English. Approximate annual cost: €700–€2,200.

Mapfre Salud

Mapfre is a major Spanish insurance group with deep national roots. Its health insurance arm offers plans across all regions of Spain and has one of the widest provider networks in the country. This makes it particularly attractive for NLV holders planning to live in smaller cities or rural areas where other providers' networks may be thinner. Approximate annual cost: €700–€2,000.

Cigna Global

Cigna operates a range of international health plans suited to expats. Some Cigna plans are accepted by Spanish consulates for NLV purposes, though you must ensure the policy specifically covers Spain comprehensively and meets the no-copayment requirement. Cigna is particularly popular with US applicants who are accustomed to American health insurance systems. Approximate annual cost: €1,200–€3,500 (international plans tend to be more expensive).

SegurCaixa Adeslas

Adeslas is one of Spain's largest domestic health insurers, backed by CaixaBank. It has an extensive network of hospitals and clinics across Spain and is frequently used by Spanish residents. Its plans are competitively priced and widely accepted for NLV applications. Adeslas can be more challenging for non-Spanish speakers to navigate, but brokers can assist with this. Approximate annual cost: €600–€1,800.

Allianz Care

Allianz Care offers international health insurance plans designed for globally mobile individuals. Some plans meet NLV requirements, but as with all international policies, you must verify the specific policy terms with your consulate or a specialist adviser. Allianz Care is popular with applicants from outside Europe who want comprehensive international coverage. Approximate annual cost: €1,000–€4,000.

How Much Does Private Health Insurance Cost in Spain?

The cost of private health insurance in Spain varies significantly based on several factors:

  • Age — the most significant factor. Younger applicants (under 40) typically pay €50–€100/month; those aged 60–70 may pay €150–€300/month
  • Number of people covered — family policies are available but the cost increases with each additional member
  • Coverage level — basic plans with a limited network cost less; comprehensive plans with broader access cost more
  • Pre-existing conditions — some insurers add loadings or exclusions for existing health conditions; others do not
  • Provider network — access to premium private hospitals (such as Hospital Quirónsalud or Clínica Universidad de Navarra) typically requires a higher-tier plan

As a general guide, a single adult aged 40–55 can expect to pay €900–€1,800 per year for a policy that meets NLV requirements. Couples may pay €1,800–€3,500 per year combined.

What to Look for When Choosing a Policy

Beyond meeting the basic NLV requirements, consider the following when selecting your health insurance:

Provider Network in Your Region

Check that the insurer has clinics and hospitals in the specific area of Spain where you plan to live. A policy with a strong Madrid network is less useful if you are living in rural Extremadura or a smaller Andalusian town. Ask the insurer for a list of contracted providers in your postcode.

English-Language Support

Most major providers have English-speaking customer service teams, but the quality varies. If you are not confident in Spanish, test the customer service line before committing to a policy.

Pre-Existing Condition Handling

This is a common sticking point for older applicants or those with existing health conditions. Some insurers cover pre-existing conditions from day one; others impose waiting periods of 6–12 months; others exclude them entirely. For NLV purposes, a policy that excludes a significant pre-existing condition may still be accepted by some consulates — but you will want to understand exactly what you are and are not covered for.

Dental and Optical Add-Ons

The NLV does not require dental or optical coverage, but these are not covered by Spain's public system either. If you have dental or vision needs, consider adding these to your policy from the outset rather than trying to add them later.

Annual vs Monthly Premium Payment

Paying annually typically saves 5–10% compared to monthly payments. Most providers offer both options. For the NLV application, you will need to show proof of coverage for the full period — an annual policy certificate makes this straightforward.
