Getting a Spanish Driving Licence as an Expat: What You Need to Know

When you need to exchange your licence, which countries have reciprocal agreements, and how the DGT process works for NLV holders.

Get Expert Help Back to Blog
Home› Blog› Spanish Driving Licence Guide

One of the more practical — but frequently overlooked — questions facing NLV holders in Spain is: what do I do with my driving licence? The answer depends on where your licence was issued, and how long you have been resident in Spain. Getting this wrong can leave you technically unable to drive legally in Spain on your foreign licence, while a simple exchange process could have resolved the issue in a matter of weeks.

This guide covers the rules for the most common nationalities of NLV holders: UK, USA, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, and EU/EEA citizens. It explains the exchange process with the DGT (Dirección General de Tráfico — Spain's traffic authority), what documents you need, the costs involved, and what happens if your country does not have a reciprocal licence exchange agreement with Spain.

The Basic Framework: When Do You Need a Spanish Licence?

The rules differ depending on whether your licence was issued by an EU/EEA country or a non-EU country.

EU and EEA Licence Holders

If your driving licence was issued by an EU or EEA member state (including Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway), it remains valid for driving in Spain indefinitely — there is no exchange deadline. Your EU licence is recognised throughout the EU under mutual recognition rules. However, if your EU licence expires while you are resident in Spain, you must renew it through the Spanish DGT rather than through your home country. After two years of Spanish residency, you may optionally exchange your EU licence for a Spanish one (which will then be renewed through Spain's system going forward).

Non-EU Licence Holders

If your licence was issued by a non-EU/EEA country — including the UK (post-Brexit), USA, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa — different rules apply. You may drive in Spain using your foreign licence for six months after you first obtain Spanish residency (i.e., after your TIE card is issued and you become a Spanish resident). After that six-month period, if you have not exchanged your licence, you are not legally permitted to drive in Spain on your foreign licence.

What happens at the end of that six months depends entirely on whether your country has a bilateral licence exchange agreement with Spain.

Countries with Reciprocal Licence Exchange Agreements

Spain has bilateral driving licence exchange agreements with a number of non-EU countries. Citizens of these countries can exchange their foreign licence for a Spanish one without taking any Spanish driving test. As of 2026, the list includes:

  • United Kingdom (see specific section below)
  • Algeria, Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, El Salvador, Japan, Morocco, Peru, Philippines, Serbia, South Korea, Tunisia, and Turkey

This list is updated periodically by the Spanish DGT through bilateral agreements signed between Spain and foreign governments. Always verify the current status of your country's agreement directly with the DGT or via the Spanish consulate in your country, as agreements can be added, modified, or suspended.

UK Licence Holders Post-Brexit: The Exchange Agreement

The situation for UK licence holders has undergone significant changes since Brexit. Before 31 December 2020, UK citizens with an EU driving licence (UK licences were EU licences) could drive in Spain on that licence indefinitely. Brexit changed this.

After a prolonged period of uncertainty during which some UK residents in Spain were effectively unable to drive legally, Spain and the UK reached a bilateral agreement for reciprocal recognition and exchange of driving licences. This agreement was approved by the Spanish Council of Ministers and published in the Spanish Boletín Oficial del Estado (BOE).

Under the current agreement, UK citizens who become Spanish residents can exchange their UK driving licence for a Spanish licence without taking a Spanish driving test. The exchange process must be completed within six months of establishing Spanish residency. During the exchange process, you will receive a temporary certificate allowing you to drive while your application is processed by the DGT.

Important points for UK licence holders:

  • The exchange is available for most standard categories (B for cars, A for motorcycles). Confirm that your specific licence category is covered by the agreement.
  • When you exchange, your UK licence is surrendered to the DGT. You can request its return when you leave Spain permanently.
  • Your exchanged Spanish licence remains valid in the UK for short visits. UK licence exchanges in Spain are generally accepted for the equivalent UK category.
  • If your UK licence has expired, present it for exchange anyway — expired licences issued during the valid period of Spanish residence can still be accepted in many cases. Confirm with the DGT.

Countries Without Exchange Agreements: Taking the Spanish Test

If your country does not have a bilateral exchange agreement with Spain — which includes the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa — you must obtain a Spanish driving licence by taking the full Spanish driving test. Your foreign licence cannot be exchanged, only recognised for the initial six-month grace period.

This is a significant undertaking for most people. The Spanish driving test consists of two parts:

The Theory Test (Examen Teórico)

The theory test covers Spanish traffic law, road signs, vehicle safety, and first aid obligations. It consists of 30 multiple-choice questions, and you must score at least 27 correct to pass. The test is taken at a DGT-approved examination centre. Most people prepare using the official DGT question bank. The test is administered in Spanish, but English-speaking candidates can request an English-language version at many test centres (availability varies by location — confirm in advance).

Study materials include the official DGT apps, online simulators, and theory courses offered by Spanish driving schools (autoescuelas). Most candidates study for 2–4 weeks using the question bank. The theory test fee is approximately €10–15 per attempt through the autoescuela.

The Practical Test (Examen Práctico)

The practical driving test is conducted on public roads with a DGT examiner. You must pass the theory test before you can attempt the practical. The practical test lasts approximately 25–35 minutes and assesses your ability to drive safely, observe traffic rules, execute manoeuvres (parking, three-point turns, emergency stops), and navigate roundabouts, motorways, and urban streets.

You must take the practical test in a vehicle from a registered autoescuela. Most driving schools offer a package for experienced drivers who simply need to pass the test: a few lessons to familiarise yourself with the Spanish car (which may have dual controls and a manual gearbox) and local road conventions, followed by the test. Packages for experienced foreign drivers typically cost €300–600 depending on the number of lessons required and your region.

You will be assigned a DGT examiner. While most tests are conducted in Spanish, some examiners can conduct the test in English or other languages in areas with high expat populations. You may also bring an official certified interpreter to the test, though this must be arranged in advance.

The DGT Exchange Process: Step by Step

For licence holders from countries with exchange agreements (including the UK), the DGT exchange process involves the following steps:

  1. Book an appointment at the DGT: Visit the DGT's website (dgt.es) to book an appointment at your provincial DGT office. In some areas you can also use a registered autoescuela to submit on your behalf, which can streamline the process.
  2. Complete the medical examination: You must present a certificate of physical and mental fitness (informe de aptitud psicofísica) from a DGT-approved medical centre (centro de reconocimiento de conductores). These centres are separate from standard GP practices — they are specialist driver medical centres. The examination costs approximately €30–50 and typically takes 20–40 minutes. The certificate is valid for a limited period, so arrange it close to your DGT appointment date.
  3. Gather your documents:
    • Your TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero) — your Spanish residency card
    • Your original foreign driving licence (it will be surrendered to the DGT)
    • Two recent passport-sized photographs
    • The medical certificate of aptitude (informe de aptitud psicofísica)
    • Proof of Spanish address (empadronamiento certificate)
    • Declaration that you do not hold a driving licence issued by another country
    • Declaration that you are not subject to any disqualification from driving in Spain or your home country
  4. Attend the DGT appointment: Submit your documents and pay the administrative fee (approximately €25–35). You will receive a temporary driving authorisation (autorizacion provisional para conducir) which allows you to drive while the Spanish licence is being issued — typically 2–4 weeks.
  5. Receive your Spanish licence: Your Spanish driving licence (permiso de conducción) will be issued and sent to your registered address or available for collection. The initial licence is valid for 10 years (under 65) or 5 years (65+).

Driving in Spain on a Foreign Licence: The Six-Month Rule in Practice

During the first six months of your Spanish residency, you can legally drive in Spain on your foreign licence. However, there are important practical points:

  • You must carry your licence with you at all times while driving. In Spain, this is a legal requirement — not having your licence on your person when pulled over can result in a fine.
  • If your foreign licence is not in a Latin script (e.g., licences from Japan, South Korea, or Morocco), you must also carry an International Driving Permit (IDP) as a translation document. IDPs must be obtained in your home country before you leave — they cannot be issued in Spain.
  • The six-month grace period begins from the date your residency is formally established — typically the date your TIE is issued. The DGT counts from the date of residency, not the date of arrival in Spain.
  • If you drive in Spain on a foreign licence beyond the six-month period without having exchanged it (where exchange is possible) or obtained a Spanish licence (where a test is required), you are driving without a valid licence in Spanish law. The fine for driving without a valid licence is €200, and you can be banned from driving during the adjudication period.

Spanish Driving Licence Categories

When you exchange your foreign licence or obtain a new Spanish licence, you will be issued the equivalent Spanish category to what you held in your home country. The main Spanish categories are:

  • B: Cars (up to 3,500 kg, up to 9 seats). This is what most people need. Minimum age 18.
  • A1: Motorcycles up to 125cc / 11 kW. Minimum age 16.
  • A2: Motorcycles up to 35 kW. Minimum age 18.
  • A: Motorcycles (unrestricted). Minimum age 24 (or 20 if you have held A2 for 2 years).
  • C: Vehicles over 3,500 kg. Minimum age 21.
  • D: Passenger vehicles (buses). Minimum age 24.

Most foreign Category B (car) licences exchange directly to Spanish Category B. If you also hold a motorcycle endorsement on your foreign licence, confirm with the DGT that this category is included in the exchange.

Costs Summary

Here is a realistic breakdown of costs for the exchange process:

  • Medical certificate: €30–50
  • DGT administrative fee: €25–35
  • Autoescuela administrative assistance (optional): €50–100
  • Total for exchange (agreement countries): approximately €80–180

For those who need to take the Spanish test:

  • Theory test preparation and exam: €100–200
  • Practical lessons and test: €300–600 (for experienced drivers)
  • Medical certificate: €30–50
  • DGT administrative fee: €25–35
  • Total for test route: approximately €500–900

Practical Tips for NLV Holders

  • Start early: Do not wait until the end of the six-month period. Medical appointments, DGT appointments, and processing times mean you should begin the process within the first 1–2 months of obtaining your TIE.
  • Keep copies of everything: Make certified copies of your foreign licence before surrendering it. If you later need to return to your home country, you will have evidence of your driving history.
  • Check your country's current agreement status: Agreements can change. Before assuming your country has an exchange agreement, verify the current position with the DGT or your immigration specialist.
  • Use an autoescuela for the test: Spanish driving schools deal with the DGT regularly and can often navigate the appointment system more efficiently than going direct. For the test route, an autoescuela is essential — the practical test must be taken in their vehicle.
  • Carry your temporary authorisation: When your licence is being processed by the DGT, carry the temporary driving authorisation with your foreign licence at all times while driving.
