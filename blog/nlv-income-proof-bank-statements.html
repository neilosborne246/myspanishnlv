NLV Income Proof: What Bank Statements Do You Need?

Bank statements are central to every NLV application — but consulates are specific about what they want. Here is exactly what to provide, how to prepare your statements, and what to do if your income is irregular or held in a foreign currency.

Start Your Application ← All Guides

Why bank statements matter for your NLV

The Non-Lucrative Visa requires you to prove you have sufficient passive income to support yourself in Spain without working. Bank statements are the primary evidence of this — they show a consulate officer, in black and white, that money is consistently arriving in your account from a source that isn't Spanish employment.

Getting your bank statement documentation right is one of the most important things you can do for a successful application. Missing statements, unsigned PDFs, or statements that show irregular deposits are among the most common reasons applications are delayed or refused.

How many months of bank statements do you need?

The standard requirement is 3–6 months of bank statements. Most consulates in the UK and Europe ask for 3 months minimum. US consulates — particularly New York, Miami, and Los Angeles — often ask for 6 months, and some will request up to 12 months of history if they want to see income consistency over a longer period.

Our strong recommendation is to provide 6 months regardless of what the minimum is for your consulate. A thicker, more consistent file is almost always better received than a thin one. It also protects you if the consulate raises any questions about one particular month's deposits.

Income threshold 2026 (400% IPREM): Approximately €2,401/month for a single applicant. Add approximately €600/month for each additional adult and approximately €300/month for each dependent child.

What do the statements need to show?

The consulate officer reviewing your application wants to see a clear pattern of regular, passive income arriving in your account. Specifically, your statements should show:

  • Your full name matching your passport exactly
  • Your account number and the bank's name/address
  • A regular pattern of incoming deposits — ideally the same or similar amount each month
  • Deposits that clearly exceed the income threshold (showing €2,500+ per month for a single applicant gives comfortable headroom)
  • No evidence of employment income from a Spanish employer

The nature of the deposits matters. Pension payments, dividend transfers, rental income, investment drawdowns — these are all acceptable and recognisable to consulate staff. Large one-off transfers are not income; they are capital movements and need to be explained separately.

Official statements vs. online PDFs

This is where many applicants make mistakes. An online banking PDF that you generate yourself by logging into your account is generally not sufficient on its own. Most consulates require one of the following:

  • Official bank-stamped statements — printed by the bank and stamped with the bank's official stamp
  • Bank letter confirming income — a letter from the bank on headed paper confirming account history, average balance, and regular income deposits
  • Certified online statements — some banks will certify a PDF printout with a stamp and signature on request

Contact your bank early in the process and ask specifically for "official bank statements for visa purposes" covering the last 6 months. Most banks can produce these within a few days, though some charge a small fee (typically £10–£30 or equivalent).

Foreign currency bank statements

If your accounts are held in GBP, USD, AUD, CAD, or another non-euro currency, the statements are still acceptable — but you need to do a little extra work.

First, your statements must be in English or translated into Spanish by a sworn translator. If your bank issues statements in English, you're fine. Second, the consulate will need to see the EUR equivalent of your income. It's good practice to include a euro conversion note in your application cover letter showing the exchange rate on a specific date and the equivalent monthly income in euros. This makes the officer's job easier and demonstrates that you understand the threshold.

Some applicants also include a printout of the exchange rate from the European Central Bank website on the date of their application. This is a nice professional touch.

What if my income is irregular?

Not everyone receives income as a neat monthly transfer. Dividend income arrives quarterly. Rental income can vary month to month. Freelance or consulting income (while the NLV prohibits working in Spain, overseas income is fine) can be lumpy. How do you handle this?

The key is to provide additional supporting documentation that explains the pattern. A dividend letter from your investment platform or company showing the annual/quarterly dividend schedule. A rental tenancy agreement showing the contracted monthly rent. An investment account statement showing the portfolio value and the regular drawdown plan.

Bank statements alone are not always enough for irregular income. Build a narrative: "I receive quarterly dividends of approximately €9,000 which average €3,000/month" — and back it up with the investment account statements showing the portfolio and dividend history.

Using savings to supplement income

If your monthly income is slightly below the threshold, savings can help make up the gap. To demonstrate savings solvency, you need statements showing a maintained balance equivalent to at least one year's required income — approximately €28,800 for a single applicant.

Your savings account statements need to be official (stamped or on letterhead) and show that the funds have been maintained over the statement period, not just deposited recently. A lump sum deposited the week before you apply will raise questions. Funds that have been present for 3–6 months are far more credible.

For more detail on using savings to qualify, see our guide to NLV savings requirements.

A bank letter vs. a bank statement — which do you need?

Ideally both. Bank statements (3–6 months, official/stamped) show the transaction history and income pattern. A bank letter or solvency certificate adds a formal, authoritative declaration from the institution confirming your account details and financial position. Together they create a compelling evidence package.

If your bank charges for the letter, it's worth paying. A €30 bank letter is cheap insurance against a rejection that costs you months of reapplication time.

Do statements need to be apostilled or notarised?

In most cases, no. Bank statements and bank letters do not typically require apostille certification — they are already official documents issued by a regulated institution. However, if a consulate specifically asks for a notarised or apostilled bank letter, they will tell you. Always follow your consulate's current documentation checklist as requirements do occasionally change.
