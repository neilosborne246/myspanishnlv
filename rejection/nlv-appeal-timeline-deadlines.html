Spain NLV Appeal Timeline: The One-Month Deadline & Processing Time

Time is critical when your NLV is rejected. Your refusal notice states the appeal available and its deadline — for an administrative appeal, normally one month from notification. Miss it and that appeal is gone. Understand the timeline, the deadline, and what to expect during the appeal process.

The One-Month Deadline (Critical)

Spanish administrative law gives you one month, counted from the day after notification, to lodge a formal appeal (recurso de reposición). This is not a guideline — it's a legal deadline, and your notice is the authority on which appeal applies. After it passes, you cannot lodge that appeal; you can still reapply.

How to Count the Month

  • Start date: The day after you were notified of the decision (Ley 39/2015, art. 30.4) — not the date printed on the letter if you were notified later. Keep proof of when you were notified.
  • Check the notice: It states the appeal available, where to lodge it and the deadline; that is the deadline to follow.
  • End date: The same date one month later

Example: You are notified of the refusal on Tuesday 10 March 2026. The month is counted from 11 March and ends on Friday 10 April 2026, the same date one month later (Ley 39/2015, art. 30.4); if the last day were a non-working day, it would move to the next working day (art. 30.5). A court appeal has two months counted from the same starting point (Ley 29/1998, art. 46.1). Your notification is what to follow.

What "Lodging an Appeal" Means

Your appeal must be formally submitted to the consulate within the deadline. "Submitting" typically means:

  • Hand-delivered to the consulate (get a receipt)
  • Sent by registered mail (with proof of posting showing the date)
  • Submitted via the consulate's online system (if they have one)

Email is not sufficient. Use registered/tracked mail or in-person submission.

Full NLV Appeal Timeline

Days 1-3: Notification & Understanding Refusal

  • Receive refusal letter
  • Read the grounds for refusal carefully
  • Understand exactly why you were refused

Days 4-10: Decision — Appeal or Reapply?

  • Assess whether appealing makes sense
  • Gather evidence of what went wrong
  • Contact a specialist (if using one)

Days 11-25: Prepare Appeal Documentation

  • Collect new or clarifying evidence
  • Write detailed response to refusal grounds
  • Address each point in refusal letter
  • Prepare supporting documents
  • Have appeal translated into Spanish (if needed)

Days 26-30: Submit Appeal

  • Final review of appeal document
  • Submit via registered mail or in person
  • Get proof of submission (dated receipt, tracking number)
  • Keep copy for your records

The Following Month: The Authority Decides

  • The authority that refused you reviews the original decision and your new evidence
  • It must decide and notify within one month (Ley 39/2015, art. 124.2)
  • If it does not, the request is treated as dismissed, and you can go to court

If You Then Go to Court

  • The court deadline runs from the day after you are notified of the decision on your request (Ley 29/1998, art. 46.4); if the request goes unanswered, take specialist advice on the court deadline
  • You cannot go to court while the request for reconsideration is pending (Ley 39/2015, art. 123.2)

How Long the Decision Takes

The legal period to decide a request for reconsideration is one month (Ley 39/2015, art. 124.2). Some authorities answer sooner; if there is no answer within the month, the request is treated as dismissed and the court route opens.

Total Timeline: Rejection to Decision

  • Filing window: up to 1 month from the day after notification (preparation happens inside it)
  • Decision period: up to 1 month from filing
  • Total: usually up to about two months for the request for reconsideration
  • Court route, if needed: a separate deadline, counted as explained above

A court appeal takes considerably longer than a request for reconsideration.

What Happens If You Miss the Deadline?

You lose the request-for-reconsideration route. A court appeal is still possible only if its own deadline — two months counted from the day after notification of the refusal (Ley 29/1998, art. 46.1) — has not expired.

If both deadlines pass, the decision is final and your option is to reapply — which you can do at any time.

How People Miss the Deadline

  • They count from the date printed on the letter rather than the day after they were notified
  • They miscount the month
  • They assume the deadline is flexible
  • They wait for consulate office hours or try to submit near the end of day

Extending the Deadline (Unlikely)

Spanish administrative law is rigid on appeal deadlines. Extensions are not typically granted. You may be able to request an extension if:

  • You can prove you didn't receive notification (very difficult)
  • Extraordinary circumstances prevented you from appealing (war, severe illness — very high bar)

Don't count on this. Treat the deadline in your notice as absolute.

Timeline Tips

  • Act immediately upon rejection. Don't wait to understand the refusal — start the clock the day you receive it
  • Calculate your exact deadline in writing (e.g., "Refusal dated April 1, deadline May 1")
  • Set calendar reminders at Day 10, 20, and 25 — this ensures you don't accidentally miss the deadline
  • Assume consulate mail is slow. Don't send appeal via regular mail the last day; send on Day 25 at latest to ensure arrival
  • Keep proof of submission. Registered mail receipt or in-person submission receipt with timestamp
  • Follow up after 4 weeks. Contact consulate to confirm your appeal was received and logged

After Appeal Decision

  • If approved: Consulate will guide you next steps (visa issuance, appointment scheduling)
  • If rejected again: You can reapply immediately or go to court; a decision on a request for reconsideration cannot itself be reconsidered (Ley 39/2015, art. 124.3)
  • If requested for more info: Deadline to submit will be specified in the request — follow it
