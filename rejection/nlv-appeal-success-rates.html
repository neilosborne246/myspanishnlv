NLV Appeal Success: When Is an Appeal Worth It?

No official figures are published on how often NLV appeals succeed, so treat any percentage you see with caution. Whether an appeal is worth it depends on why you were refused — and on whether you can show the decision was wrong on the evidence the authority had.

What an Appeal Can and Can't Do

An NLV refusal can be challenged by asking the authority that decided to reconsider (recurso de reposición, within one month counted from the day after you were notified) or by a court appeal (within two months from the same day). Your refusal notification states the routes and deadlines — follow it. See how to appeal.

An appeal reviews the decision. It is not a second chance to add evidence: facts or documents you could have provided during the procedure but didn't may not be taken into account (Ley 39/2015, art. 118.1). That is why appeals suit decisions where the authority misread or overlooked what was in your file, while gaps in your evidence are usually better fixed by applying again.

Judging Your Case by Refusal Reason

Health insurance

If your policy met the requirements and the refusal misread it, an appeal can make sense. If the policy didn't meet them — co-payments, an excess, the wrong start date or cover that isn't valid in Spain — replace it and reapply. See health insurance refusals and choose a compliant policy.

Document problems (apostille, translation, format)

If your documents were valid and correctly presented, an appeal can say so. If an apostille or sworn translation was missing, a corrected new application is usually quicker and cleaner than an appeal.

Financial means

If the authority miscalculated — for example by ignoring savings, which count towards the requirement (RD 1155/2024, art. 62.1) — an appeal can address it. If your means genuinely fell short, reapply once they meet the requirement. See income requirements.

Criminal record

This depends entirely on the record and its circumstances. Take specialist advice before deciding.

Procedural issues

If you believe the procedure wasn't followed — for example a notification that wasn't properly made — an appeal is the route, and specialist advice is essential.

Appeal or Reapply? A Quick Guide

  • The authority misread or overlooked evidence you supplied: consider an appeal.
  • Evidence was missing, out of date or insufficient: reapplying with the corrected file is usually better.
  • Your circumstances have changed since you applied: reapply.
  • You're unsure: check your deadline first, then take advice. See appeal or reapply.

Questions to Ask Before You Decide

  • Do you have the full refusal notification, with its reasons and the routes it offers?
  • Was the decision wrong on the evidence the authority had?
  • Could you have provided the missing evidence before the decision?
  • Which deadlines are still open?
  • Would a corrected new application be faster for you?
