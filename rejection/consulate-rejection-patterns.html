NLV Refusals and Your Consulate: Why Procedures Differ

Spanish consulates apply the same regulation, but each publishes its own checklist and appointment process, and the documents it asks for can differ. No official refusal rates by consulate are published, so be wary of "strict" and "lenient" league tables. What lowers your risk is following your own consulate's current list exactly.

What Differs Between Consulates

  • The checklist: each consulate publishes its own NLV document list. The Spanish consulate in London's checklist, for example, accepts a registered S1 receipt as health insurance; other consulates set their own lists.
  • Appointments: some consulates book directly, others through an external provider such as BLS International.
  • Practical rules: how many copies to bring, which documents need sworn translation, and how recent certificates must be.
  • Which office covers you: you apply to the consulate responsible for where you live. See our consulate directory.

What Doesn't Differ

The legal requirements are the same everywhere: sufficient means (€2,400 a month for the main applicant in 2026, plus €600 for each family member), health insurance, and no criminal record (RD 1155/2024, arts. 61–62). The consulate checks your means and insurance, and the immigration office in Spain decides on the residence authorisation (arts. 39 and 63) — so the outcome isn't set by one consulate's habits alone. Common Refusal Reasons Everywhere

  • Means that are insufficient or poorly evidenced — see income and savings
  • Health insurance that doesn't meet the requirements — see the insurance requirement
  • Missing apostilles or sworn translations
  • Certificates that are out of date by the appointment
  • Documents missing from the consulate's checklist

See common reasons for refusal.

How to Prepare for Your Consulate

  • Download the current checklist from your consulate's official website shortly before you apply.
  • Check which consulate covers your address.
  • Book your appointment early and time your certificates so they are still valid on the day.
  • If something on the list is unclear, ask the consulate in writing and keep the reply.
